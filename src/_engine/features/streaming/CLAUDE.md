Notes for world streaming (`src/_engine/features/streaming/`, `aekasha/streaming`: the cells, their state machine and content, and the Streaming debug tab); the root `.claude/CLAUDE.md` keeps the project-wide rules, commands and workflow.

## Streaming

The `streaming()` feature (`StreamingFeature.ts`, p353, docs/plans/_DONE_p353_macro-streaming-grid.md): a scene larger than what fits in memory at once, cut into square cells on x/z whose content loads and unloads by its distance to a focus. Phases 1-5 are in: cells, the state machine, code-registered content, asset ownership per cell, priming, the scene JSON's content cut into cells at build time, the cells' physics bodies (their tiers and the hand-over of the ones that move), and far representations (a cell's three content layers, the fades, the JSON far content).

- **Settings.** A scene opts in with `streaming: { cellSize, rings?, margin?, nearbyPhysicsTier?, fadeSeconds? }` in its JSON (`StreamingSchema.ts`' `streamingSchemaPart`, so the feature manifest names `streaming`) or `setSceneStreamingSettings(sceneId, settings)` (over the JSON's; `null` off, `undefined` back to the JSON's; read at the scene's `created` hook, so from its scene code works). `cellSize` is a power of two of at least 32 m (p306's blocks tile it); `rings` each state's outermost ring in cells (Chebyshev from the focus cell), `resident` 1, `nearby` 2, `far` 5 by default, `resident <= nearby <= far`; `margin` defaults to `cellSize / 4`; `nearbyPhysicsTier` (`STATIC` default, or `DISABLED`; `STREAMING_NEARBY_PHYSICS_TIERS`) is a `NEARBY` cell's bodies' tier (see Bodies); `fadeSeconds` (0 or more, finite; 0 = no fades) the fades' length, by default `AppConfig.lod.fadeSeconds` read at the install, else `DEFAULT_STREAMING_FADE_SECONDS` (0.25; `resolveStreamingSettings(settings, defaultFadeSeconds)`, see Fades). The checks are `StreamingSettings.ts`' (no zod: the runtime and the schema's refine share them, and a build ships no schema). The budgets are `AppConfig.streaming` (`updateIntervalFrames` 10, `maxConcurrentLoads` 2, `maxCreateMsPerFrame` 2, `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` 2, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` and `maxShownObjectsPerFrame` by the backend), read at the install.
- **The cell maths** is `src/_engine/utils/CellKey.ts` (moved out of `spatial()` in Phase 1: a feature imports no other feature's code); a cell key is `packCellKey(cx, 0, cz)`, made by `getStreamingCellKey(cx, cz)` / `getStreamingCellKeyAt(x, z, cellSize)` (which throw outside the key's range).
- **Levels** (`StreamingRings.ts`, pure): `RESIDENT`, `NEARBY`, `FAR`, `UNLOADED`. `decideStreamingLevel` promotes a cell to the nearest level whose ring holds it and demotes it only once the focus is more than `rings[level] × cellSize + margin` from its edge (`getCellEdgeDistance`, Chebyshev in metres), to the nearest level that still keeps it: a focus on a boundary never thrashes. `DEEPEST_LOADED_LEVEL` is `FAR`: the cells to the far ring are tracked, a scene with only near content tracking its `FAR` cells with empty layers (a few hundred cells decided every N frames: microseconds).
- **Layers** (p353 Phase 5 D1-D3, `StreamingLayers.ts`): a cell's content is in three layers, by content range (`STREAMING_LAYER_RANGES`' order, kept, near, far): `NEAR_AND_FAR` (kept from `FAR` in: a cell's ground), `NEAR` (`RESIDENT` and `NEARBY`: its full content) and `FAR` (only while `FAR`: its stand-ins); `isRangeWanted(range, level)`. Each layer runs the machine below on its own units (phase, units, generation, in flight, late units, priming, owner key, adopted bodies, fade), so `NEARBY` ↔ `FAR` loads one layer and unloads the other and the kept one never moves. A layer the level no longer wants waits, drawn, until every layer the level wants is `LOADED` (`releaseLeavingLayers`, run when the level changes and when a layer is shown; D2): one never shown (`LOADING`, `PRIMING`) unloads at once, a leaving one wanted again before its fade out starts just stays (its generation unchanged), and an incoming one still `LOADING` is cancelled. A cell's `phase` (D3, `updateStreamingCellPhase`, what `getStreamingCellState` reads) is its wanted layers' least advanced (`UNLOADING` counting as `UNLOADED`, so a switching cell whose incoming layer is queued reads `UNLOADED`, the tab's `QUEUED`, while it shows its leaving one), `LOADED` once they're all shown, and with nothing wanted `UNLOADING` while a layer has content. An empty layer is shown at once with one notification, isn't counted (loads, unloads, cancels) and isn't released. `getStreamingCellLayer(cell, range)`.
- **The machine** (`StreamingCells.ts`, the cells: levels, decisions, the pump's order and budgets, the scene load, the drop; `StreamingLayers.ts`, a layer's units, loading, priming, unloading, fades and release; no engine state; its tests drive it with their own clock): per layer `UNLOADED → LOADING → PRIMING → LOADED(level) → UNLOADING → UNLOADED` (`PRIMING` only for a layer streamed in during play with entities), with a generation counter bumped by every load start, cancel and drop. `decideStreamingCells` (every `updateIntervalFrames`) moves the tracked cells and starts tracking the untracked ones within the loaded rings, nearest first; `pumpStreamingCells` (every frame) starts loads up to `maxConcurrentLoads` (cells with a layer `LOADING`; a cell already loading starts its other queued layers), runs their content units nearest first (kept before near before far within a cell), a frame of the primings and the fades, and unloads farthest first (the other way round), the two sharing `maxCreateMsPerFrame`, checked before each unit (a frame always runs one of each, and can go over by one unit). A load no longer wanted is cancelled: its units not started are skipped, what's done unloads, and an async unit that comes back late (a stale generation) is unloaded at once (`lateResults`). A cell wanted again while it unloads finishes the unload, then loads again. A unit whose `load` throws, rejects or returns no id array is logged and counted (`unitErrors`), and the cell still loads. `setStreamingTransitionListener` (one listener, `@internal`) is the debug tab's cell history: the machine pays a null check.
- **Content** (`StreamingContent.ts`): `registerStreamingCellContent({ sceneId, cellKey, load, unload?, loadedAt? })` (`Streaming.ts`, with the install check, `loadedAt` among `STREAMING_CONTENT_RANGES`) adds a unit to its cell's layer of `loadedAt` (public `StreamingContentRange`, default `'NEAR'`); `load(ctx)` returns the ids of the entities it made (or a promise of them), `unload(ids, ctx)` takes them down (default: deletes them). `ctx` (`StreamingCellContext`) has the cell, the world and `isCurrent()` (false once the load is cancelled or the scene left: checked after every `await`). A unit is the slice a frame can't split, so a big cell is many units. A scene's registrations go on its exit, so its scene code registers them on every load (before its cells load); a cell already loaded takes a new unit on its next load. `unregisterStreamingCellContent` takes the same object.
- **JSON content** (p353 Phase 3, the plan's D1-D7; Phase 5 D7-D9 for its layers): the gather (`devTools/gather/streamingManifest.ts`, `devTools/gather/CLAUDE.md`) cuts a streaming scene's resolved meshes and point and spot lights into the cells their positions are in, and writes them to the scene's `__streaming` (`StreamingManifest.ts`, public types on `aekasha/schemas`: each entry and asset once, a cell holding indices, its x/z `bounds` and `bytesEstimate`, its cell-only textures' and imports' `__vramBytes.out`); the scene's own lists keep its base. An asset only cell content references (a mesh's `geo`, `mat`, `matOverrides`, `lod` levels; a material's texture ids, an atlas cell input's slot `<atlas>.<slot>` among them (`addTextureRefs` reads that form's dependency: the slot's id is no string in the value); an import by its `<id>/<node>` geometries) moves into the cells with it; one the base, `backgroundTexture` or a material that stays base uses, or that nothing references, stays base, so scene code gets only base assets. `streaming.base` (ids kept base), `streaming.radii` (radii by mesh app id, for an import's geometry the gather can't measure) and `streaming.farEntries` (below) are build time only: the output drops them, `setSceneStreamingSettings` warns once and ignores them. A mesh whose half extent is over `cellSize / 2` (an unrotated box's own sides, else a primitive's bounding sphere, else `radii`) goes into every cell it overlaps, in its kept layer (D8: one entity alive while any of its cells is loaded at any level, its own `lod` drawing its coarse levels; a far copy beside the near one would z-fight). **Far entries** (D7; for kit meshes p377 D1-D4, `docs/plans/_DONE_p377_hlod-streaming-cell-proxies.md`): a cell's mesh (not kept) whose resolved `lod` has explicit `levels` gets one, its coarsest level (its omitted `geo` / `mat` / `castShadow` from the levels before it) at the mesh's transform; one without `lod` (a kit mesh: a wall piece, a pillar, rubble, usually merged `AUTO` near) gets itself, only when it groups (below; one that wouldn't gets none: a far mesh of its own is a draw a mesh, no fewer than its near content). Either without `lod`, `appId` and `merge` (a far copy never joins a merge group). None with a `cullScreenSize` over 0 (so a mesh that should vanish at a distance takes `lod: { levels: [{ screenSize: 0 }], cullScreenSize }`, which costs a `LOD` near), `AUTO` (its chain isn't known at build time) or for a light. Per scene, `streaming.farEntries`: `'ALL'` (default) or `'LOD_LEVELS'` (explicit levels only, the rule before engine 6.9.0; `getFarMeshProps`). Per cell, the far entries with the same string `geo` and `mat`, no `matOverrides` and the same two shadows are one group (`manifest.farMeshes`: its `props`, the four fields, and `entries`, the indices of its near entries in the manifest's `meshes`, whose `position`, `rotation` (or `quaternion`) and `scale` are its instances: an index in the first download instead of a second transform, p377 D3; coarsest levels and meshes' own entries alike; `instances` of transforms is `@deprecated`, still drawn when a group has no `entries`, removed in engine 7.0.0), the others meshes of their own (their props with the transform). A group is one `InstancedMesh`, as cheap as the cell's content merged into one mesh per material (p377 Phase 0: within 0.2 ms at 400 ruin sites). A kit's far layer lists the kit's geometries, materials and textures in every far cell (once in memory, a cache hit while a near layer has them; held while any tracked cell is loaded). Its near content merged, a near ↔ far switch draws the merge group whole while the far group dithers (a merge group doesn't fade with a cell, `features/merging/CLAUDE.md`'s Streaming): accepted (p377 D5). **Per layer** (D9, backwards compatible): a cell's own lists and `bytesEstimate` are its near layer's, and its optional `far` and `kept` (`StreamingManifestLayer`) the others'; an asset moves into each layer whose entries reference it (a cell's near and far layers can both list one: each loads it in its own owner key, the second a cache hit). At runtime, `sceneRegistered` keeps each scene's manifest, and `created` loads `StreamingDataContent.ts` with an `import()` only for a scene with one (0.8 KiB on demand) and checks it: a `cellSize` set in code other than the manifest's fails the load, streaming off in code warns that the cell content won't load. A layer's data units (`rt.dataContent(cellKey, range)`, ahead of its code units, cached per `cellKey/range`): an async unit loading the layer's cell-only assets (`loadSceneAssetsAsync` with the layer's owner key, `SceneDataLoader.ts`), then its entries 8 a unit, the first `waits` (the machine starts no waiting unit while the layer has units in flight). The near layer's entries are its lights and own meshes (`createSceneObject3Ds`); the far layer's its far entries, a group as `createFarGroupEntity(world, far, manifest.meshes)` (one `InstancedMesh` of the registered geometry and material, an instance matrix per entry from its near entry's props, or from `instances`, its bounds over every instance, a ref on both, `userData.entityId`, `OBJECT3D` and `TAG_IS_MESH`, whose delete hook disposes it and releases the refs; in the root scene, not in the ECS frustum culling: three culls it by those bounds; it throws naming a missing asset, so the unit fails and is counted), a mesh of its own through `createSceneObject3Ds`; the kept layer's a unit per shared (oversized) mesh, per scene by its manifest index: the first holder creates it, every holder's unit returns its id, and the last to unload deletes it. **Shared entities** (found in Phase 5 section 5: with only the first holder returning the id, the wall faded out when its first cell left and popped back, and the last cell deleted it unfaded): the data content is the runtime's `sharedEntities` (`getHolders(entityId)`, the holders in load order), and the machine (`getDrawnEntityIds`) lets only the first holder hold, prime, fade in and release it, and fade it out only once no other holder's cell still wants its kept layer (the first of them). A first holder cancelled while priming it, others still holding, shows it unprimed; one fading it out when another cell loads it again shows it again after the fade: both rare, neither counted. Point and spot lights stream too, but adding or removing a light changes three's lighting of every lit material (not measured; the test scene has none).
- **Fades** (p353 Phase 5 D4-D6, `StreamingFades.ts`' `createStreamingFader(onMaterialDithered)`, loaded on demand with the machine, the runtime's injected `fader`): every entity with an Object3D a layer shows during play dithers in over `fadeSeconds`, and one it takes down dithers out first (its unit's `unload` runs once the layer's fade ends). The fade is the kernel's second per-object fade (`kernel/render/LodFade.ts`' visibility fade, `setObjectVisibilityFade`, `@internal`: the per-object `vec2` uniform's y, ANDed with the LOD's in the same dither, so a LOD cross-fade during a streaming fade composes exactly; a LOD fade copy reads its mesh's; the shadow pass fades too), so streaming imports no LOD code. `fader.prepare(world, ids, hidden)` dithers every material of a unit's entities' meshes (`enableLodDither`) when they arrive, before any draw (`finishUnit`, before the priming's `hold`), hidden for a layer streamed in during play (`layer.fadesIn`: its groups show invisible and the layer fades in together); `start` collects the meshes once (nothing per frame allocates) and draws the first frame; `draw` writes `progress` (in) or `progress - 1` (out), and at 1 clears the fades, an out fade then holding its entities (`TAG_DRAW_HELD`) until they're taken down; `release` lets go of what a content's own `unload` leaves alive. The layer's `fade` (`{ job, direction, startedAt, ids }` on the runtime's clock): `showLayer` starts the fade in, `releaseLeavingLayers` the fade out of a shown leaving layer once the wanted layers are shown (the same call, so a near ↔ far switch fades both at once) and turns a wanted layer's fade out back in (a new job on the same ids at `1 - progress`, `fadesReversed`), and the pump draws each after the priming and starts a fade out's unload when it ends. A layer with no entities has nothing to fade and unloads at once. Fades run only with a fader and `fadeSeconds` over 0 (the machine's tests' runtimes have none). A scene load's cells are prepared but shown with the scene, and the scene's exit drops everything unfaded. A body handed to a shown layer (`showAdoptedBody`) is let go of a fade or hold its old layer left on it; one handed to a layer not shown yet takes its fade in. `unfadedTakeDowns` counts a shown layer with entities taken down during play without its fade out while fades are on (an exit check: 0). A material that changes is new to the primer (`forgetPrimedMaterial`, the fader's `onMaterialDithered`), so its dithered build is compiled before it's drawn. Trap: a material the scene's base drew before a cell first fades it rebuilds on its next draw for every mesh using it (a cold compile): give such materials `enableLodDither` in the scene code. Not faded: an entity without its own Object3D (an instanced LOD pool's instance: its pool's meshes are shared, and the priming doesn't hold one either).
- **Code content's shared assets go in each unit's `load`.** An asset is deleted on its last ref (`decGeometryRef` and the material's count, unless persistent), so a geometry or material the scene code makes once and only cells use is gone once no cell holds it, and the next cell's load fails. Get it by id in each unit's `load` (`createMaterial` / `createGeometry` with an id are a cache hit while it exists, adding the cell as an owner, and make it again after): `streamingGrid` does (`getSharedAssets`). JSON content doesn't need it: a cell-only asset loads with every cell that names it.
- **Priming** (p353 §5, `StreamingPriming.ts`, the runtime's injected `primer`; the measurements behind it are the plan's Phase 0 and Phase 2 section 3): a cell streamed in during play is never drawn before it's primed. Each finished unit's entities with an Object3D get `TAG_DRAW_HELD` (the kernel's draw hold, in `reconcileObject3DVisibility`'s AND, so culling and LOD can't show them), and once the cell's units are all in it's `PRIMING`: its held meshes' materials' textures not uploaded by a priming before go through `renderer.initTexture` while the frame is under `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` (shared by the cells; an uncompressed one over 1024² gets a frame of its own, `oversizedUploads`); then, when a held mesh has a material no priming showed before, one `preWarmObjectsAsync` of the held objects (the kernel's, `src/_engine/kernel/render/CLAUDE.md`'s Pre-warm compiles: the real objects in one call, never stand-ins, since three releases a pipeline with its last render object); then the meshes of shown materials go at once and the rest a material at a time, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` a frame across the cells (WebGL2 1, WebGPU 8 by default: WebGL2 still pays ~10 ms per new structure's first draw, primed or not). Every held object is let go of under `maxShownObjectsPerFrame` too, across the cells (WebGL2 16, WebGPU not paced: WebGL2's first draw of an object costs GPU-process time even for a known shader, and a column of cells changing level at once, ~10 layers, took 33-37 ms frames; p353 Phase 5 section 5), so a layer may be let go of over frames (a group's `next`); it's drawn invisible until its fade in starts, with its last object, so it never shows in parts (without a fader, it does). Each object let go of draws its first frame unculled (`frustumCulled` off until the next `beginFrame`, one its owner set off left so), so its first draw is paid in its paced frame: one off screen would otherwise pay it when it comes into view (the flight's 180° turn to cells never drawn: 22 first draws in one 46 ms frame). "New" is by material object (`shownMaterials`, a WeakSet): a new material of a known structure is compiled again (6-50 ms, no hitch) and paced, which costs time only. A cell the focus reaches (`RESIDENT`) while it primes is shown at once and counted (`primingStalls`: widen the rings). A scene load's cells aren't primed (shown with the scene, the load screen hides their first draws), nor a cell without entities. An async unit's entities are held when it resolves, so one that creates entities and then awaits more can show them cold: create them at its end. A content's own `unload` that leaves entities alive has them let go (`primer.release`).
- **Asset ownership** (p353 §4.2; the kernel side is `src/_engine/kernel/scene/CLAUDE.md`'s Asset ownership): each layer has its owner key (Phase 5 D10, `layer.ownerKey`): the near layer `sceneId#cellKey` (Phases 2-4's), the far `sceneId#cellKey/far`, the kept `sceneId#cellKey/kept` (`getCellOwnerKey(sceneId, cellKey, layer?)`, `getStreamingLayerOwnerLayer(range)`), so a layer's release can't free an asset another layer of its cell is still loading, and a cell's GPU memory sums its three keys (the GPU memory tab's `getMemoryOwner` lists an asset only one cell's layers hold under the cell's near key). Each unit's `load` runs in its layer's key (`runInAssetOwnerScope`) up to its first `await`, an asset load it starts there registers in it after its own awaits, and `ctx.runOwned(fn)` puts code after an `await` in it. Once a layer's unload is done, `finishStreamingLayerUnload` calls the runtime's `releaseCellAssets(cellKey, layer?)` (`releaseCellOwnedAssets` with the layer): what the layer alone held and nothing uses is freed (`assetReleases`, `assetsReleased`). A cancelled load's async units still out hold it back (`layer.lateUnits`, counted at the cancel; the last one to settle releases), and so does another cell object tracking the key (a load of it may be running): its own unload releases. The scene's exit releases nothing (`isDropped`): SceneLoader's `releaseSceneOwnedAssets` takes the cells' keys with the scene's, and when the same scene loads again (SceneLoader releases nothing then), `created` releases the dropped cells the new load didn't load again under their three keys (`droppedCells`). A release scans every registry (3-5 µs at `streamingGrid`'s 17 assets, linear in the assets registered). Mesh entities' ref counts free most of a code unit's assets anyway (a mesh's geometry and material on their last ref, the material's textures with it): the release is for what no entity holds, a texture a cancelled unit loaded before bailing out, an import's record and unused geometries, and Phase 3's JSON assets.
- **Bodies** (p353 Phase 4, the plan's D1-D9; per layer since Phase 5 D11; `StreamingBodies.ts`, `createStreamingBodies(nearbyTier)`, one per scene's machine as `rt.bodies`, loaded by `created` with an `import()` for a scene that streams (1.5 KiB on demand, so the feature's first download stays under its 6 KiB budget); the machine's own tests use a stand-in with `locateMover` and, for the hand-over, `handOver: handOverMovedBodies`). A body is an entity in `BODY_DYNAMIC_VISUAL` / `BODY_DYNAMIC_HEADLESS` or with `PHYSICS_SIM_TIER` (kernel component keys: streaming imports no physics code, and without `physics()` nothing is one). **Hand-over** (D1, instead of the plan's `STREAMING` spatial domain; `StreamingHandOver.ts`' `handOverMovedBodies`, the bodies' `handOver` hook): when a layer starts unloading (`beginStreamingLayerUnload`, also a cancelled `LOADING` one), each body it holds (its units' ids and `layer.adopted`) is placed by its `TRANSFORM` x/z; one inside another tracked cell whose layer of the same range is `LOADING`, `PRIMING` or `LOADED` (`isLayerHolding`) goes to that layer's `adopted` (a near crate rolls into the next cell's near layer) (leaving its unit's ids, so a custom `unload` never sees it; `bodiesAdopted`), the rest go with the cell. A layer deletes its adopted bodies after its units (and on the scene's exit; `deleteAdoptedBodies`); a deleted one is dropped from the list. **Rebalance** (found in review: a crate the plough pushed along stayed its pile's cell's, and went `DISABLED` beside the plough when that cell left `RESIDENT`): every decision starts with the bodies' `rebalance` (`decideStreamingCells`), which moves each body the cells hold to the same layer of the loaded cell it's in (`findAdopter` returns a layer, `takeBodyFromLayer`, the layer's `adopted`) and requests the moved ones' tiers per cell, so a body's tier is the cell it's in; the hand-over at unload covers the moves since the last decision. A few hundred position reads every `updateIntervalFrames`. **Tiers** (D2, the frame path: not deterministic, like the policy's `FRAMES`): through the kernel's `PhysicsTierAccess` (`kernel/ecs/PhysicsTierAccess.ts`, set by `physicsTiers()`), one batch per event: a unit finishing (`unitLoaded`), a cell with content getting another level (`levelChanged`, from `setCellLevel`, for every holding layer, a leaving one's too), a hand-over (`adopted`). A body's want is its layer's (`getLayerWant`): a layer its cell no longer wants keeps `nearbyTier` until it's deleted (a `REMOVED` round trip for a fade's length is waste); else `RESIDENT` `FULL`, or `POLICY` for a body that wants the policy; `FAR` `REMOVED` (the kept and far layers' bodies); otherwise `nearbyTier`. Physics gives a joint group its finest want (a jointed group `STATIC` for `REMOVED`, as for `DISABLED`: physics' Simulation tiers), and leaves a group with a `POLICY` member to the policy. A `REMOVED → FULL` refusal for capacity leaves the body `REMOVED` in a `RESIDENT` cell, which `summarize`'s off-tier count shows. Before the batch, the cell's policy bodies go out of the policy (not `RESIDENT`) or back in (`setPolicyMember`, D5). **The creation scope** (D3): each unit's `load` (to its first `await`) and `ctx.runOwned` run in `runInCellScopes` (the asset owner key, then `runInPhysicsCreationScope` with `getCreationScope`): `createPhysicsEntity` makes a `DYNAMIC` body in the layer's tier (`initialTier`, in its own create message; `DISABLED` where the want is `REMOVED`, which `initialTier` has no value for, the unit's batch then asking `REMOVED`), and a `tierPolicy` one in a cell that isn't `RESIDENT` joins nothing and is reported to the cell (`holdPolicyMember`). Characters pass `initialTier: 'FULL'`. A layer forgets its bodies when its unload starts (`forgetLayer`, after the hand-over). Without `physicsTiers()`: no scope, no request, one warning. A unit returns its bodies' ids once their creates have resolved (an entity without its body isn't a body yet). `summarize(world)` counts them per tier, those held out of the policy, and those off their cell's tier, characters and jointed bodies left out (`PhysicsTierAccess.isJointed`: a joint group takes its members' finest want, so a chain lying into a `NEARBY` cell stays `FULL` with its `RESIDENT` half), and `STATIC` counting as a `DISABLED` cell's (a jointed body is never `DISABLED`, see physics' Simulation tiers), each body against its layer's want (the tab, checks): a layer that started unloading has forgotten its bodies, so for the frames its units take to unload the world holds more than the cells count. The no-tiers warning names `NEARBY` or `FAR`.
- **Runtime** (`Streaming.ts`): `streamingSystem` (default world, `MAIN`) decides and pumps the current scene's machine, never during a load (`loadStart` → `loadSettled`). The scene hooks: `sceneRegistered` keeps the JSON settings, `exit` drops every cell (their unloads run, in reverse install order before physics' entities) and the scene's registrations, `created` loads the machine (`StreamingCells.ts`, `StreamingLayers.ts`, `StreamingPriming.ts`, `StreamingFades.ts`, `StreamingBodies.ts`, and `StreamingDataContent.ts` for a scene with a manifest) with `import()`s, builds the runtime and loads the cells around the focus at once (every layer their level wants), with no budget, awaited, so they're part of the load (inside physics' hold, p101). `Streaming.ts` imports the machine only as types: the feature's first download is the API, the registry and the scene hooks (`yarn sbp`'s streaming group, budget 4 KiB; the rest on demand, Phase 5 D12). The focus (`setStreamingFocus`: an entity's `TRANSFORM` position or a point read at every decision; `null`, the default, the main camera) lasts across loads; while its entity doesn't exist the cells keep their states. `getStreamingCellState(cellKey)` reads a cell's state in the current scene. While the determinism probe is armed (`isDeterminismProbeArmed`, `kernel/config/DeterminismProbe.ts`, read only behind `IS_DEBUG_ENV`) the system makes no decisions: the cells stay where the scene's load put them, so a probe run streams nothing.
- **Debug** (`debug/`, registered with `if (IS_DEBUG_ENV) ctx.onDebug(registerStreamingDebugGUI)`): the Streaming tab (`_dbg__Streaming.ts`, `streamingControls`, after LOD in `DEFAULT_DEBUG_DRAWER_TAB_ORDER`, icon `streaming`; nothing persisted): the scene's settings (`fadeSeconds` too), the focus and its cell, the cells per state (`FAR` among them), the machine's counters, the priming's (primes, stalls, uploads, oversized uploads, compiles, new materials shown), the fades (running in and out, started, turned around, `unfadedTakeDowns`), the cells' bodies (per tier, handed over, held out of the policy, off their cell's tier: `summarize`), the cells' GPU memory (total, the largest cell, and per layer: near, far, kept), the JSON content (the manifest's cells, entries and far meshes, its far groups and their instances (`entries` or `instances`) apart from the meshes of their own, the tracked cells' estimate (each cell's kept layer and its near or far one by its level), the far groups alive and their instances (`countFarGroups`), the oversized meshes alive; `getStreamingDataContent`), Freeze focus and Teleport focus to cell (the focus override, `setStreamingFocusOverride`, over the app's focus while set), the budgets live, the overlay and a row per tracked cell (its GPU bytes in the subtitle) opening its window (`_dbg__StreamingCellWindow.ts`, a window kind removed on a scene change: the cell's state, ring, its layers (each one's phase, units, generation, running fade and its progress, owner key and GPU memory), its far groups and instances, the longest layer's load and priming times, entities, its JSON content per layer from the manifest (known before it loads), GPU memory, the assets its layers' owner keys are on, and last 12 transitions). The bytes and assets are `_dbg__StreamingMemory.ts`' (the GPU memory tab's `collectGPUAssets` by `getMemoryOwner`: an asset several cells share counts under the scene, one a cell's layers share under the cell; `getCellGPUBytes` sums a cell's three keys), read once per refresh. The overlay (`_dbg__StreamingOverlay.ts`) draws the tracked cells' outlines at a set height, a line per state colour (far orange, priming purple; an empty line hidden), and the focus as a cross; without `lines()` it's off with one log line. `?streamingFocus=x,z` sets the override before the first load (read in the install, only in the debug env and prod test mode).
- **Limits:** an `AUTO` LOD's mesh gets no JSON far entry (a follow-up: its chain at build time), nor a mesh without `lod` that wouldn't group (an inline `geo` or `mat`, or `matOverrides`: give it a registered `geo` and `mat`, or explicit levels), nor a light (adding or removing one changes every lit material); an instanced LOD pool's instances aren't faded, nor an instance set's ranges (a cell adds and removes its range: load such cells from a ring past the set's cull distance, `features/instancing/CLAUDE.md`); an imported model's or a `MERGED` geometry's size needs `streaming.radii` (p371: a mesh with one is placed as a point; its parts' refs come with it); a cell's state isn't saved across an unload (§8: a `REMOVED` body keeps its state only while its entity exists, so through `FAR`). A far cell's bodies change the near simulation slightly (accepted, p353 Phase 5): its `FIXED` colliders (a ground slab: they have no tier) and its jointed groups (`STATIC`, never `REMOVED`) stay in Rapier's world, and its broad phase then orders contact pairs differently (`streamingPhysics`: after 300 steps 184 of the near cells' 198 bodies bit-identical with and without the far ring, the plough's pile up to 8.5 mm apart). Still deterministic: the probe hash is the same in every worker target for one set of rings, and changes with them. Taking a far cell's level colliders out of the world would be a physics change of its own.
