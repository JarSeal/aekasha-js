Notes for world streaming (`src/_engine/features/streaming/`, `aekasha/streaming`: the cells, their state machine and content, and the Streaming debug tab); the root `.claude/CLAUDE.md` keeps the project-wide rules, commands and workflow.

## Streaming

The `streaming()` feature (`StreamingFeature.ts`, p353, docs/plans/p353_macro-streaming-grid.md): a scene larger than what fits in memory at once, cut into square cells on x/z whose content loads and unloads by its distance to a focus. Phases 1-4 are in: cells, the state machine, code-registered content, asset ownership per cell, priming, the scene JSON's content cut into cells at build time, and the cells' physics bodies (their tiers and the hand-over of the ones that move); far representations are later.

- **Settings.** A scene opts in with `streaming: { cellSize, rings?, margin?, nearbyPhysicsTier? }` in its JSON (`StreamingSchema.ts`' `streamingSchemaPart`, so the feature manifest names `streaming`) or `setSceneStreamingSettings(sceneId, settings)` (over the JSON's; `null` off, `undefined` back to the JSON's; read at the scene's `created` hook, so from its scene code works). `cellSize` is a power of two of at least 32 m (p306's blocks tile it); `rings` each state's outermost ring in cells (Chebyshev from the focus cell), `resident` 1, `nearby` 2, `far` 5 by default, `resident <= nearby <= far`; `margin` defaults to `cellSize / 4`; `nearbyPhysicsTier` (`STATIC` default, or `DISABLED`; `STREAMING_NEARBY_PHYSICS_TIERS`) is a `NEARBY` cell's bodies' tier (see Bodies). The checks are `StreamingSettings.ts`' (no zod: the runtime and the schema's refine share them, and a build ships no schema). The budgets are `AppConfig.streaming` (`updateIntervalFrames` 10, `maxConcurrentLoads` 2, `maxCreateMsPerFrame` 2, `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` 2, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` by the backend), read at the install.
- **The cell maths** is `src/_engine/utils/CellKey.ts` (moved out of `spatial()` in Phase 1: a feature imports no other feature's code); a cell key is `packCellKey(cx, 0, cz)`, made by `getStreamingCellKey(cx, cz)` / `getStreamingCellKeyAt(x, z, cellSize)` (which throw outside the key's range).
- **Levels** (`StreamingRings.ts`, pure): `RESIDENT`, `NEARBY`, `FAR`, `UNLOADED`. `decideStreamingLevel` promotes a cell to the nearest level whose ring holds it and demotes it only once the focus is more than `rings[level] × cellSize + margin` from its edge (`getCellEdgeDistance`, Chebyshev in metres), to the nearest level that still keeps it: a focus on a boundary never thrashes. `DEEPEST_LOADED_LEVEL` is `NEARBY` until Phase 5's far representations, so a `FAR` cell has no content yet.
- **The machine** (`StreamingCells.ts`, no engine state; its tests drive it with their own clock): per cell `UNLOADED → LOADING → PRIMING → LOADED(level) → UNLOADING → UNLOADED` (`PRIMING` only for a cell streamed in during play with entities), with a generation counter bumped by every load start, cancel and drop. `decideStreamingCells` (every `updateIntervalFrames`) moves the tracked cells and starts tracking the untracked ones within the loaded rings, nearest first; `pumpStreamingCells` (every frame) starts loads up to `maxConcurrentLoads`, runs their content units nearest first and unloads farthest first, the two sharing `maxCreateMsPerFrame`, checked before each unit (a frame always runs one of each, and can go over by one unit). A load no longer wanted is cancelled: its units not started are skipped, what's done unloads, and an async unit that comes back late (a stale generation) is unloaded at once (`lateResults`). A cell wanted again while it unloads finishes the unload, then loads again. A unit whose `load` throws, rejects or returns no id array is logged and counted (`unitErrors`), and the cell still loads. `setStreamingTransitionListener` (one listener, `@internal`) is the debug tab's cell history: the machine pays a null check.
- **Content** (`StreamingContent.ts`): `registerStreamingCellContent({ sceneId, cellKey, load, unload? })` (`Streaming.ts`, with the install check) adds a unit; `load(ctx)` returns the ids of the entities it made (or a promise of them), `unload(ids, ctx)` takes them down (default: deletes them). `ctx` (`StreamingCellContext`) has the cell, the world and `isCurrent()` (false once the load is cancelled or the scene left: checked after every `await`). A unit is the slice a frame can't split, so a big cell is many units. A scene's registrations go on its exit, so its scene code registers them on every load (before its cells load); a cell already loaded takes a new unit on its next load. `unregisterStreamingCellContent` takes the same object.
- **JSON content** (p353 Phase 3, the plan's D1-D7): the gather (`devTools/gather/streamingManifest.ts`, `devTools/gather/CLAUDE.md`) cuts a streaming scene's resolved meshes and point and spot lights into the cells their positions are in, and writes them to the scene's `__streaming` (`StreamingManifest.ts`, public types on `aekasha/schemas`: each entry and asset once, a cell holding indices, its x/z `bounds` and `bytesEstimate`, its cell-only textures' and imports' `__vramBytes.out`); the scene's own lists keep its base. An asset only cell content references (a mesh's `geo`, `mat`, `matOverrides`, `lod` levels; a material's texture ids; an import by its `<id>/<node>` geometries) moves into the cells with it; one the base, `backgroundTexture` or a material that stays base uses, or that nothing references, stays base, so scene code gets only base assets. `streaming.base` (ids kept base) and `streaming.radii` (radii by mesh app id, for an import's geometry the gather can't measure) are build time only: the output drops them, `setSceneStreamingSettings` warns once and ignores them. A mesh whose half extent is over `cellSize / 2` (an unrotated box's own sides, else a primitive's bounding sphere, else `radii`) goes into every cell it overlaps. At runtime, `sceneRegistered` keeps each scene's manifest, and `created` loads `StreamingDataContent.ts` with an `import()` only for a scene with one (0.8 KiB on demand) and checks it: a `cellSize` set in code other than the manifest's fails the load, streaming off in code warns that the cell content won't load. A cell's data units (`rt.dataContent`, ahead of its code units): an async unit loading its cell-only assets (`loadSceneAssetsAsync` with the cell's owner key, `SceneDataLoader.ts`), then its lights and own meshes 8 entries a unit (`createSceneObject3Ds`), the first `waits` (the machine starts no waiting unit while the cell has units in flight), then a unit per shared (oversized) mesh, counted per scene by its manifest index: the first holder creates it and primes it, the last to unload deletes it. Point and spot lights stream too, but adding or removing a light changes three's lighting of every lit material (not measured; the test scene has none).
- **Code content's shared assets go in each unit's `load`.** An asset is deleted on its last ref (`decGeometryRef` and the material's count, unless persistent), so a geometry or material the scene code makes once and only cells use is gone once no cell holds it, and the next cell's load fails. Get it by id in each unit's `load` (`createMaterial` / `createGeometry` with an id are a cache hit while it exists, adding the cell as an owner, and make it again after): `streamingGrid` does (`getSharedAssets`). JSON content doesn't need it: a cell-only asset loads with every cell that names it.
- **Priming** (p353 §5, `StreamingPriming.ts`, the runtime's injected `primer`; the measurements behind it are the plan's Phase 0 and Phase 2 section 3): a cell streamed in during play is never drawn before it's primed. Each finished unit's entities with an Object3D get `TAG_DRAW_HELD` (the kernel's draw hold, in `reconcileObject3DVisibility`'s AND, so culling and LOD can't show them), and once the cell's units are all in it's `PRIMING`: its held meshes' materials' textures not uploaded by a priming before go through `renderer.initTexture` while the frame is under `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` (shared by the cells; an uncompressed one over 1024² gets a frame of its own, `oversizedUploads`); then, when a held mesh has a material no priming showed before, one `preWarmObjectsAsync` of the held objects (the kernel's, `src/_engine/kernel/render/CLAUDE.md`'s Pre-warm compiles: the real objects in one call, never stand-ins, since three releases a pipeline with its last render object); then the meshes of shown materials go at once and the rest a material at a time, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` a frame across the cells (WebGL2 1, WebGPU 8 by default: WebGL2 still pays ~10 ms per new structure's first draw, primed or not). "New" is by material object (`shownMaterials`, a WeakSet): a new material of a known structure is compiled again (6-50 ms, no hitch) and paced, which costs time only. A cell the focus reaches (`RESIDENT`) while it primes is shown at once and counted (`primingStalls`: widen the rings). A scene load's cells aren't primed (shown with the scene, the load screen hides their first draws), nor a cell without entities. An async unit's entities are held when it resolves, so one that creates entities and then awaits more can show them cold: create them at its end. A content's own `unload` that leaves entities alive has them let go (`primer.release`).
- **Asset ownership** (p353 §4.2; the kernel side is `src/_engine/kernel/scene/CLAUDE.md`'s Asset ownership): a cell's owner key is `sceneId#cellKey` (`cell.ownerKey`). Each unit's `load` runs in it (`runInAssetOwnerScope`) up to its first `await`, an asset load it starts there registers in it after its own awaits, and `ctx.runOwned(fn)` puts code after an `await` in it. Once a cell's unload is done, `finishUnload` calls the runtime's `releaseCellAssets` (`releaseCellOwnedAssets`): what the cell alone held and nothing uses is freed (`assetReleases`, `assetsReleased`). A cancelled load's async units still out hold it back (`cell.lateUnits`, counted at the cancel; the last one to settle releases), and so does another cell object tracking the key (a load of it may be running): its own unload releases. The scene's exit releases nothing (`isDropped`): SceneLoader's `releaseSceneOwnedAssets` takes the cells' keys with the scene's, and when the same scene loads again (SceneLoader releases nothing then), `created` releases the dropped cells the new load didn't load again (`droppedCells`). A release scans every registry (3-5 µs at `streamingGrid`'s 17 assets, linear in the assets registered). Mesh entities' ref counts free most of a code unit's assets anyway (a mesh's geometry and material on their last ref, the material's textures with it): the release is for what no entity holds, a texture a cancelled unit loaded before bailing out, an import's record and unused geometries, and Phase 3's JSON assets.
- **Bodies** (p353 Phase 4, the plan's D1-D9; `StreamingBodies.ts`, `createStreamingBodies(nearbyTier)`, one per scene's machine as `rt.bodies`, loaded by `created` with an `import()` for a scene that streams (1.5 KiB on demand, so the feature's first download stays under its 6 KiB budget); the machine's own tests use a stand-in with `locateMover` and, for the hand-over, `handOver: handOverMovedBodies`). A body is an entity in `BODY_DYNAMIC_VISUAL` / `BODY_DYNAMIC_HEADLESS` or with `PHYSICS_SIM_TIER` (kernel component keys: streaming imports no physics code, and without `physics()` nothing is one). **Hand-over** (D1, instead of the plan's `STREAMING` spatial domain; `StreamingHandOver.ts`' `handOverMovedBodies`, the bodies' `handOver` hook): when a cell starts unloading (`beginUnload`, also a cancelled `LOADING` one), each body it holds (its units' ids and `cell.adopted`) is placed by its `TRANSFORM` x/z; one inside another tracked cell that is `LOADING`, `PRIMING` or `LOADED` goes to that cell's `adopted` (leaving its unit's ids, so a custom `unload` never sees it; `bodiesAdopted`), the rest go with the cell. A cell deletes its adopted bodies after its units (and on the scene's exit; the machine's `deleteAdoptedBodies`); a deleted one is dropped from the list. **Tiers** (D2, the frame path: not deterministic, like the policy's `FRAMES`): through the kernel's `PhysicsTierAccess` (`kernel/ecs/PhysicsTierAccess.ts`, set by `physicsTiers()`), one batch per event: a unit finishing (`unitLoaded`), a cell with content getting another loaded level (`levelChanged`, from `setCellLevel`), a hand-over (`adopted`). A body's want is its cell's: `RESIDENT` `FULL`, or `POLICY` for a body that wants the policy; otherwise `nearbyTier`; physics gives a joint group its finest want, and leaves a group with a `POLICY` member to the policy. Before the batch, the cell's policy bodies go out of the policy (not `RESIDENT`) or back in (`setPolicyMember`, D5). **The creation scope** (D3): each unit's `load` (to its first `await`) and `ctx.runOwned` run in `runInCellScopes` (the asset owner key, then `runInPhysicsCreationScope` with `getCreationScope`): `createPhysicsEntity` makes a `DYNAMIC` body in the cell's tier (`initialTier`, in its own create message), and a `tierPolicy` one in a cell that isn't `RESIDENT` joins nothing and is reported to the cell (`holdPolicyMember`). Characters pass `initialTier: 'FULL'`. A cell forgets its bodies when its unload starts (`forgetCell`, after the hand-over). Without `physicsTiers()`: no scope, no request, one warning. A unit returns its bodies' ids once their creates have resolved (an entity without its body isn't a body yet). `summarize(world)` counts them per tier, those held out of the policy, and those off their cell's tier (the tab, checks): a cell that started unloading has forgotten its bodies, so for the frames its units take to unload the world holds more than the cells count.
- **Runtime** (`Streaming.ts`): `streamingSystem` (default world, `MAIN`) decides and pumps the current scene's machine, never during a load (`loadStart` → `loadSettled`). The scene hooks: `sceneRegistered` keeps the JSON settings, `exit` drops every cell (their unloads run, in reverse install order before physics' entities) and the scene's registrations, `created` builds the machine and loads the cells around the focus at once, with no budget, awaited, so they're part of the load (inside physics' hold, p101). The focus (`setStreamingFocus`: an entity's `TRANSFORM` position or a point read at every decision; `null`, the default, the main camera) lasts across loads; while its entity doesn't exist the cells keep their states. `getStreamingCellState(cellKey)` reads a cell's state in the current scene. While the determinism probe is armed (`isDeterminismProbeArmed`, `kernel/config/DeterminismProbe.ts`, read only behind `IS_DEBUG_ENV`) the system makes no decisions: the cells stay where the scene's load put them, so a probe run streams nothing.
- **Debug** (`debug/`, registered with `if (IS_DEBUG_ENV) ctx.onDebug(registerStreamingDebugGUI)`): the Streaming tab (`_dbg__Streaming.ts`, `streamingControls`, after LOD in `DEFAULT_DEBUG_DRAWER_TAB_ORDER`, icon `streaming`; nothing persisted): the scene's settings, the focus and its cell, the cells per state, the machine's counters, the priming's (primes, stalls, uploads, oversized uploads, compiles, new materials shown), the cells' bodies (per tier, handed over, held out of the policy, off their cell's tier: `summarize`), the JSON content (the manifest's cells and entries, the tracked cells' `bytesEstimate`, the oversized meshes alive; `getStreamingDataContent`), Freeze focus and Teleport focus to cell (the focus override, `setStreamingFocusOverride`, over the app's focus while set), the budgets live, the overlay and a row per tracked cell (its GPU bytes in the subtitle) opening its window (`_dbg__StreamingCellWindow.ts`, a window kind removed on a scene change: the cell's state, ring, load and priming times, units, entities, its JSON content from the manifest (known before it loads), GPU memory, the assets its owner key is on, and last 12 transitions). The bytes and assets are `_dbg__StreamingMemory.ts`' (the GPU memory tab's `collectGPUAssets`: an asset several cells share counts under the scene), read once per refresh. The overlay (`_dbg__StreamingOverlay.ts`) draws the tracked cells' outlines at a set height, a line per state colour (priming purple; an empty line hidden), and the focus as a cross; without `lines()` it's off with one log line. `?streamingFocus=x,z` sets the override before the first load (read in the install, only in the debug env and prod test mode).
- **Not yet** (p353 Phase 5): `FAR` content, and with it a cell's `REMOVED` bodies (and the `DISABLED` fallback for jointed ones).
- The test scene is the app's `streamingGrid` (`src/app/scenes/streamingGrid/`): 16 × 16 cells of 64 m, each a terrain block (its own geometry, the cell's asset) and two units of scattered props on one world-space height field, plus JSON content (Phase 3): a beacon (a pillar textured with `testTexture`, a cap) in every fourth cell, whose materials, geometries and texture only the cells load, and a 320 m wall across six cells (an oversized mesh); the main camera flies across the grid, hovers on a cell boundary or stays, and each cell's crates can bring a shader of their own (`streamingGridContent.uniqueCrateShaders`, the scene tab's "A shader per cell": priming tests). The physics test scene is `streamingPhysics` (`src/app/scenes/streamingPhysics/`, on the simulation lint): 12 × 12 cells of 64 m, each a ground slab (a `FIXED` collider), a 14-crate pile in the tier policy near its +x edge and, on row 0, a four-link jointed chain lying across that edge; a kinematic plough mows row 0 on fixed steps (the streaming and policy focus, reset to the main camera on exit) and pushes crates over the edges; `nearbyPhysicsTier: 'DISABLED'`, so the tint tells the cell's tier (grey) from the policy's (orange `FULL`, blue `STATIC`). p353's measurements are in the plan (Phases 1-4).
