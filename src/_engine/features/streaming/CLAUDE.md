Notes for world streaming (`src/_engine/features/streaming/`, `aekasha/streaming`: the cells, their state machine and content, and the Streaming debug tab); the root `.claude/CLAUDE.md` keeps the project-wide rules, commands and workflow.

## Streaming

The `streaming()` feature (`StreamingFeature.ts`, p353, docs/plans/p353_macro-streaming-grid.md): a scene larger than what fits in memory at once, cut into square cells on x/z whose content loads and unloads by its distance to a focus. Phases 1-2 are in: cells, the state machine, code-registered content, asset ownership per cell and priming; far representations, the build-time manifest and per-cell physics tiers are later.

- **Settings.** A scene opts in with `streaming: { cellSize, rings?, margin? }` in its JSON (`StreamingSchema.ts`' `streamingSchemaPart`, so the feature manifest names `streaming`) or `setSceneStreamingSettings(sceneId, settings)` (over the JSON's; `null` off, `undefined` back to the JSON's; read at the scene's `created` hook, so from its scene code works). `cellSize` is a power of two of at least 32 m (p306's blocks tile it); `rings` each state's outermost ring in cells (Chebyshev from the focus cell), `resident` 1, `nearby` 2, `far` 5 by default, `resident <= nearby <= far`; `margin` defaults to `cellSize / 4`. The checks are `StreamingSettings.ts`' (no zod: the runtime and the schema's refine share them, and a build ships no schema). The budgets are `AppConfig.streaming` (`updateIntervalFrames` 10, `maxConcurrentLoads` 2, `maxCreateMsPerFrame` 2, `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` 2, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` by the backend), read at the install.
- **The cell maths** is `src/_engine/utils/CellKey.ts` (moved out of `spatial()` in Phase 1: a feature imports no other feature's code); a cell key is `packCellKey(cx, 0, cz)`, made by `getStreamingCellKey(cx, cz)` / `getStreamingCellKeyAt(x, z, cellSize)` (which throw outside the key's range).
- **Levels** (`StreamingRings.ts`, pure): `RESIDENT`, `NEARBY`, `FAR`, `UNLOADED`. `decideStreamingLevel` promotes a cell to the nearest level whose ring holds it and demotes it only once the focus is more than `rings[level] × cellSize + margin` from its edge (`getCellEdgeDistance`, Chebyshev in metres), to the nearest level that still keeps it: a focus on a boundary never thrashes. `DEEPEST_LOADED_LEVEL` is `NEARBY` until Phase 5's far representations, so a `FAR` cell has no content yet.
- **The machine** (`StreamingCells.ts`, no engine state; its tests drive it with their own clock): per cell `UNLOADED → LOADING → PRIMING → LOADED(level) → UNLOADING → UNLOADED` (`PRIMING` only for a cell streamed in during play with entities), with a generation counter bumped by every load start, cancel and drop. `decideStreamingCells` (every `updateIntervalFrames`) moves the tracked cells and starts tracking the untracked ones within the loaded rings, nearest first; `pumpStreamingCells` (every frame) starts loads up to `maxConcurrentLoads`, runs their content units nearest first and unloads farthest first, the two sharing `maxCreateMsPerFrame`, checked before each unit (a frame always runs one of each, and can go over by one unit). A load no longer wanted is cancelled: its units not started are skipped, what's done unloads, and an async unit that comes back late (a stale generation) is unloaded at once (`lateResults`). A cell wanted again while it unloads finishes the unload, then loads again. A unit whose `load` throws, rejects or returns no id array is logged and counted (`unitErrors`), and the cell still loads. `setStreamingTransitionListener` (one listener, `@internal`) is the debug tab's cell history: the machine pays a null check.
- **Content** (`StreamingContent.ts`): `registerStreamingCellContent({ sceneId, cellKey, load, unload? })` (`Streaming.ts`, with the install check) adds a unit; `load(ctx)` returns the ids of the entities it made (or a promise of them), `unload(ids, ctx)` takes them down (default: deletes them). `ctx` (`StreamingCellContext`) has the cell, the world and `isCurrent()` (false once the load is cancelled or the scene left: checked after every `await`). A unit is the slice a frame can't split, so a big cell is many units. A scene's registrations go on its exit, so its scene code registers them on every load (before its cells load); a cell already loaded takes a new unit on its next load. `unregisterStreamingCellContent` takes the same object.
- **Priming** (p353 §5, `StreamingPriming.ts`, the runtime's injected `primer`; the measurements behind it are the plan's Phase 0 and Phase 2 section 3): a cell streamed in during play is never drawn before it's primed. Each finished unit's entities with an Object3D get `TAG_DRAW_HELD` (the kernel's draw hold, in `reconcileObject3DVisibility`'s AND, so culling and LOD can't show them), and once the cell's units are all in it's `PRIMING`: its held meshes' materials' textures not uploaded by a priming before go through `renderer.initTexture` while the frame is under `maxPrimeMsPerFrame` (shared by the cells; an uncompressed one over 1024² gets a frame of its own, `oversizedUploads`); then, when a held mesh has a material no priming showed before, one `preWarmObjectsAsync` of the held objects (the kernel's, `src/_engine/kernel/render/CLAUDE.md`'s Pre-warm compiles: the real objects in one call, never stand-ins, since three releases a pipeline with its last render object); then the meshes of shown materials go at once and the rest a material at a time, `maxNewStructuresPerFrame` a frame across the cells (WebGL2 1, WebGPU 8 by default: WebGL2 still pays ~10 ms per new structure's first draw, primed or not). "New" is by material object (`shownMaterials`, a WeakSet): a new material of a known structure is compiled again (6-50 ms, no hitch) and paced, which costs time only. A cell the focus reaches (`RESIDENT`) while it primes is shown at once and counted (`primingStalls`: widen the rings). A scene load's cells aren't primed (shown with the scene, the load screen hides their first draws), nor a cell without entities. An async unit's entities are held when it resolves, so one that creates entities and then awaits more can show them cold: create them at its end. A content's own `unload` that leaves entities alive has them let go (`primer.release`).
- **Asset ownership** (p353 §4.2; the kernel side is `src/_engine/kernel/scene/CLAUDE.md`'s Asset ownership): a cell's owner key is `sceneId#cellKey` (`cell.ownerKey`). Each unit's `load` runs in it (`runInAssetOwnerScope`) up to its first `await`, an asset load it starts there registers in it after its own awaits, and `ctx.runOwned(fn)` puts code after an `await` in it. Once a cell's unload is done, `finishUnload` calls the runtime's `releaseCellAssets` (`releaseCellOwnedAssets`): what the cell alone held and nothing uses is freed (`assetReleases`, `assetsReleased`). A cancelled load's async units still out hold it back (`cell.lateUnits`, counted at the cancel; the last one to settle releases), and so does another cell object tracking the key (a load of it may be running): its own unload releases. The scene's exit releases nothing (`isDropped`): SceneLoader's `releaseSceneOwnedAssets` takes the cells' keys with the scene's, and when the same scene loads again (SceneLoader releases nothing then), `created` releases the dropped cells the new load didn't load again (`droppedCells`). A release scans every registry (3-5 µs at `streamingGrid`'s 17 assets, linear in the assets registered). Mesh entities' ref counts free most of a code unit's assets anyway (a mesh's geometry and material on their last ref, the material's textures with it): the release is for what no entity holds, a texture a cancelled unit loaded before bailing out, an import's record and unused geometries, and Phase 3's JSON assets.
- **Runtime** (`Streaming.ts`): `streamingSystem` (default world, `MAIN`) decides and pumps the current scene's machine, never during a load (`loadStart` → `loadSettled`). The scene hooks: `sceneRegistered` keeps the JSON settings, `exit` drops every cell (their unloads run, in reverse install order before physics' entities) and the scene's registrations, `created` builds the machine and loads the cells around the focus at once, with no budget, awaited, so they're part of the load (inside physics' hold, p101). The focus (`setStreamingFocus`: an entity's `TRANSFORM` position or a point read at every decision; `null`, the default, the main camera) lasts across loads; while its entity doesn't exist the cells keep their states. `getStreamingCellState(cellKey)` reads a cell's state in the current scene.
- **Debug** (`debug/`, registered with `if (IS_DEBUG_ENV) ctx.onDebug(registerStreamingDebugGUI)`): the Streaming tab (`_dbg__Streaming.ts`, `streamingControls`, after LOD in `DEFAULT_DEBUG_DRAWER_TAB_ORDER`, icon `streaming`; nothing persisted): the scene's settings, the focus and its cell, the cells per state, the machine's counters, the priming's (primes, stalls, uploads, oversized uploads, compiles, new materials shown), Freeze focus and Teleport focus to cell (the focus override, `setStreamingFocusOverride`, over the app's focus while set), the budgets live, the overlay and a row per tracked cell (its GPU bytes in the subtitle) opening its window (`_dbg__StreamingCellWindow.ts`, a window kind removed on a scene change: the cell's state, ring, load and priming times, units, entities, GPU memory, the assets its owner key is on, and last 12 transitions). The bytes and assets are `_dbg__StreamingMemory.ts`' (the GPU memory tab's `collectGPUAssets`: an asset several cells share counts under the scene), read once per refresh. The overlay (`_dbg__StreamingOverlay.ts`) draws the tracked cells' outlines at a set height, a line per state colour (priming purple; an empty line hidden), and the focus as a cross; without `lines()` it's off with one log line. `?streamingFocus=x,z` sets the override before the first load (read in the install, only in the debug env and prod test mode).
- **Not yet** (later phases of p353): the determinism probe doesn't freeze the focus (Phase 4 adds the kernel seam it needs; Phase 1's content has no physics).
- The test scene is the app's `streamingGrid` (`src/app/scenes/streamingGrid/`): 16 × 16 cells of 64 m, each a terrain block (its own geometry, the cell's asset) and two units of scattered props on one world-space height field; the main camera flies across the grid, hovers on a cell boundary or stays, and each cell's crates can bring a shader of their own (`streamingGridContent.uniqueCrateShaders`, the scene tab's "A shader per cell": priming tests). p353 Phase 1's measurements are in the plan.
