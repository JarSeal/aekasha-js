# Techniques

Long-form how-to pages: how to build something with Ækasha from the source file to the running scene, with what it costs and what it suits. The Ækasha Hub's feature pages say what each API does and link here for the workflow; these pages say how to use it in a real project.

Most pages are about texturing terrain and large structures (the terrain texturing epic, p301). Two are general:

- [Asset optimization](asset-optimization.md): the KTX2 and meshopt pipeline, its profiles and slots, pack channels, texture arrays and atlases, and the codec cheat sheet. Every texture on the other pages goes through it.
- [Writing Ækasha Hub pages](hub-authoring.md): authoring the Hub.

## Start here

New to terrain in Ækasha? Read in this order:

1. [Asset optimization](asset-optimization.md), its [Add a texture](asset-optimization.md#add-a-texture) and [Pack channels](asset-optimization.md#pack-channels): how a source image becomes a compressed GPU texture, and how one texture is packed from several maps.
2. [Texture sets](texture-sets.md): the surfaces themselves, packed maps with their real-world size. Pick one from the toolkit's library of 16, or add your own.
3. Open the gallery: `yarn dev`, then `/?isDebug=true&startScene=terrainGallery`. Every library set on its own block, and a row of blocks per technique, each signposted; its debug tab ("Terrain gallery") lists the rows, switches packings, tiers and debug views, lays a 1 m checker over the ground, puts the camera where each game type's would be (Camera presets) and measures a block's GPU time ([below](#how-the-figures-are-measured)). The Procedural row has the toolkit's ten surface generators, baked or live, with their params as sliders ([Procedural textures](procedural-textures.md): surfaces made in the engine, for when no scan fits or there's no texture budget).
4. [Single-layer terrain](terrain-single-layer.md): the terrain material itself (`createTerrainMaterial`, or the material `terrain`), the two routes, a layer's settings, anti-tiling, the quality tiers and the debug views, which every other technique page builds on.
5. **Pick your camera:** find your game type in the [matrix](#which-technique-for-my-game) and read the pages its row links, in its order: the blend first (vertex colour, splat map or rules), then the cliffs' projection, then the far surface. The second table's row names the weather and the dressing pages, and what that ground measured from that camera. The techniques combine in one material: paint or rules for the weights, projection on the cliff layer, a colour map or a far surface past a distance. In the gallery, the tab's Camera presets put you behind that camera on any block.
6. The terrain's geometry: blocks shaped and painted in Blender with the terrain add-on ([Terrain blocks from Blender](terrain-blocks-blender.md), the workflow every technique's Blender section starts from), or generated by the engine ([Procedural terrain meshes](terrain-procedural-meshes.md)).
7. Hand-built levels rather than a heightfield: [Modular kits and tile-chunk worlds](terrain-modular-kits.md).
8. Dressing the ground: pebbles, rocks and leaf litter on the blocks, placed by their paint or by Blender ([Terrain scatter](terrain-scatter.md)).
9. Unique detail where it matters: tracks, cracks, stains and footprints, from Blender with the blocks or stamped during play ([Terrain decals](terrain-decals.md)).
10. The weather: wet, puddled, rained on, snowed over and frozen ([Wet and dry surfaces](wet-dry-surfaces.md)).
11. All of it together: the showcase, `/?isDebug=true&startScene=terrainShowcase`. Four 64 m blocks (a coast and an alpine slope painted in Blender, wetlands with a bunker, and a generated block beside the alpine one, authored against generated), with their scatter and decals, a character to walk them, and its tab's rain shower, snowfall and day. Each block is a page's worked example; the page's "Template file" section names it.

## Which technique for my game

The packing and texel density columns are [Texture sets](texture-sets.md)'s, the technique, anti-tiling and projection columns the terrain material's pages' (p305; the tiers are `getTerrainQualityPreset`'s). The texel density is what the camera shows of a metre of ground near it (screen pixels a metre at 1080p: `1080 / (2 · distance · tan(fov / 2))`), so a set's texture beyond it is wasted there.

What the measurements changed in the recommendations:

- **The packing is memory, not GPU time.** `FULL`, `LITE` and `MINIMAL` blocks cost the same within 0.02 ms at every tier ([Texture sets](texture-sets.md#performance)): choose by the VRAM and the download, and by what the look needs (`MINIMAL`'s bump, `FULL`'s own AO).
- **The tiers are the GPU's knob:** `LOW` halves a many-layer material (sparse top 2, no anti-tiling), `HIGH` adds `HEX` on the dominant layer for 0.1–0.3 ms, `ULTRA` costs about twice `HIGH` (1.8–2.8 ×). `MEDIUM` and `HIGH` are the targets; `ULTRA` is for close-up hero shots.
- **The far surface pays from above and far away, little in the third person:** 70 % off a 64 m block seen from 50 m up, 0.1 ms off in a third-person view where most of the ground is nearer than its start ([Colour map + detail](terrain-colormap-detail.md#performance)). It's an open world's saving, as the tiers' 120–200 m starts say.
- **The weather costs nothing measurable in the wettest state** (+0.00 to +0.02 ms over dry) and 0.04–0.27 ms under full snow ([Wet and dry surfaces](wet-dry-surfaces.md#performance)): `SIMPLE` is a low-end GPU's choice, not a default.
- **Static dressing** (p372's merging): a block's props, rocks placed by hand and cut-out decals merge into a group per material (`merge: 'AUTO'` groups by cell), one draw each per pass. The gallery doesn't merge (its decal stage draws 24 quads unmerged, about 0.15 ms); merging is measured on the CPU side, where it pays: `largeWorld`'s 409 pieces from 381 draws to 38 a pass and its CPU frame a third lower ([Modular kits](terrain-modular-kits.md#performance)), which is also the page for worlds built from modules or tiles.

## How the figures are measured

The gallery's measurement (its tab's Measurement section, `terrainGallery`'s debug tab): a block alone (the others hidden, the ground and the sky left) in a pose, the frame's GPU time from the engine's frame record (`onGpuFrameTime` on `aekasha/debug`), the mean of 120 frames after 30, first with the block hidden and then shown at each quality tier its row takes, with the frame's draw calls. **The block's figure is the frame less the frame without it.** The pose is the measurement's own (the block across the canvas from above) or a camera preset's (the tab's Pose select; the flight preset standing at its flyover's point 60 m before the block); **Measure matrix** runs the matrix above. The pixel ratio is 1 and the sky stands still while it measures; the gallery casts no shadows and has no PostFX. Each table's heading names the GPU, the browser and the canvas: figures compare within one machine only, so measure yours (WebGPU: on WebGL2 three times about two frames in three).

The figures on these pages: p310, 2026-11-20, Windows Chrome 157 on an AMD RDNA 3 GPU, WebGPU, 1920 × 1080, unless a page names another run (the plans' own, eg. p305's at 1280 × 800).

## The page template

Every technique page has these sections, in this order. A section that doesn't apply says so in a line rather than being left out (the texture sets page has no Blender template file, the modular kits page no maps of its own).

1. **What it is.** Two or three sentences, and a diagram when it helps.
2. **Best for:** the art style (photoreal, stylized PBR, painterly, low-poly flat), the game type and camera (first person, third person, top-down, isometric / RTS, side-view platformer, racing / flight) and the world size. A "poor fit" row is required.
3. **Performance:** fetches per pixel (the formula below, with a worked example), ALU notes, VRAM, draw calls, the quality tier, and the **measured** GPU time from the gallery ([How the figures are measured](#how-the-figures-are-measured)).
4. **Maps:** a required / optional table with the packing, colour space, codec and size.
5. **Blender, step by step.**
6. **Export:** the exact exporter (or image) settings.
7. **Import into Ækasha:** the files to create, with JSON examples.
8. **Set-up and parameters:** a table.
9. **Variations:** each with what to do, why, and its performance effect.
10. **Pitfalls and checklist.**
11. **Template file and example asset ids.**

### The performance formula

Every page states its cost in the same terms (p301 §6). Texture fetches per pixel ≈ `P × L × A × T + S + M + W`:

| Term | Meaning                      | Values                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `P`  | Textures per layer (packing) | `FULL` 3, `LITE` 2, `MINIMAL` 1                                                                                                                                                               |
| `L`  | Layers actually sampled      | All layers, or the top few with [sparse sampling](terrain-splat-map.md#sparse-sampling)                                                                                                       |
| `A`  | Anti-tiling taps             | None 1, dual-scale 2, hex 3                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `T`  | Projection                   | UV / world 1, biplanar 2, triplanar 3 (slope-gated: only on steep pixels)                                                                                                                     |
| `S`  | Splat fetches                | 0 for vertex colours and rules, 1 per 4 layers for splat maps                                                                                                                                 |
| `M`  | Macro variation              | 1                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `W`  | Wet effects                  | 0 for the wetness alone; 1 for puddle patches, 1 for the rain occlusion map; gated by the weather: 2 ripples, 1–2 streaks, `P` snow ([Wet and dry surfaces](wet-dry-surfaces.md#performance)) |

`LITE`, 4 layers, dual-scale anti-tiling, world UVs, a splat map and macro variation: 2·4·2·1 + 1 + 1 = **18**, a good default. `FULL`, 4 layers, hex, always triplanar: 3·4·3·3 + 2 = **110**: don't.

The binding budget matters as much: WebGPU gives a shader stage 16 sampled textures by default, and shadow maps and the environment map take some, so a terrain material binds about 10 at most (3 `FULL` or 4 `LITE` layers with a splat and a macro texture). Past that, a texture set array binds one array per slot (2–3 bindings for up to 16 layers; [Texture sets' Variations](texture-sets.md#set-arrays)).
