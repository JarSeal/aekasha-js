Terrain starts with the surfaces: dry sand, a grey cliff, lake ice. A texture set is one
surface as a few packed PBR maps plus what a material needs to draw it at the right size: the
metres one tile covers, the metres of relief its height stands for, and defaults for its tint,
normal strength and roughness. Materials name a set by id. The toolkit ships 16 of them, CC0 scans
from ambientCG and Poly Haven, compressed to KTX2, and a baseline material that draws one. A set
can also be generated: ten procedural surfaces, baked into a set's textures at load, exported as
an ordinary set, or run live in the material.

The terrain material puts the sets on the ground. One material built from a config blends up to 16
layers by vertex colour, splat maps or rules of slope, height and facing, breaks up their tiling,
projects the cliffs, samples only the layers that matter at each pixel, and switches to a colour
map or a flat far surface in the distance. Only what the config turns on is compiled, and quality
tiers trade the techniques for speed.

Terrain blocks are the ground itself: square heightfield grids that a scene lists and spawns as one
unit each, mesh, material, its own splat or colour map and collider together, from a
`*.terrainBlock.json`. A block is shaped and painted in Blender with Ækasha's terrain add-on and
its templates, or generated by the engine from seeded noise, a heightmap or your own function, its
rules laid into its vertex colours, on a worker so the frame doesn't wait.

A block's scatter dresses it: pebbles, rocks, boulders and leaf litter drawn as instances in
cells, placed by the block's own paint and slope or by Blender's Geometry Nodes, seeded per block,
and owned by the block. The toolkit brings procedural and scanned rocks in a material that
matches the ground and gets wet with it.

Decals add the unique detail no tiling has: tyre tracks, cracks, stains, scorch marks and
footprints, each a small mesh UV'd into a cell of a decal atlas and drawn over the ground in a
decal material that gets wet with it, its ruts and prints filling with water first. A block's
decals are made in Blender and spawn with it; during play a pool stamps footprints and tyre
tracks that follow the ground exactly, all of them one render object, the oldest recycled.

::: scene terrainGallery
Every set of the toolkit's library on a 12 m block of generated terrain with a 35° ramp, the cliffs
with a wall, under a still 10:00 sky. Its debug tab ("Terrain gallery") flies the camera to a
set, switches `sand01`, `grass01` and `cliffs01` between their three packings, and lays a 1 m
checker over the ground for judging each set's scale. Around the library, a row per technique of
the terrain material (anti-tiling, splatting, rules, cliffs, colour maps, structures, a live
procedural layer), each with its sliders and measurement, the Procedural row: each generator on a
block, baked, live or beside an imported set of its category, and the Scatter row: procedural and
imported rocks on one block, and leaf litter beside it, and the Decals row and stage: both decal
atlases, and every cell on mud and concrete, blended and cut out. The tab's Quality and Debug
view selects switch every terrain block to a tier or a debug view.
